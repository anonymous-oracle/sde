                                      Basic RAG Workflow

Motivation: LLMs can benefit from access to external knowledge sources for factual accuracy and
reliability.

How (Basic) RAG Works:
Ø Input: User provides a query.
Ø Retrieval: Relevant documents are retrieved from a knowledge source (e.g., Wikipedia).
Ø Generation: The LLM uses the retrieved information to generate a response.

Benefits:
Factual Consistency: Improves the accuracy of generated content.
Up-to-Date Information: Allows LLMs to access current information beyond their training data.

TF-IDF
tf-idf (term frequency-inverse document frequency)

Measure of importance of words in a document

 ●   term frequency
       ○ How frequent a term appears in a document
       ○ More frequent words are aligned with the topic of the document

 ●   inverse document frequency
       ○ Inversely proportional to the number of documents containing the term
       ○ Terms present in many documents are not significant

Agentic RAG
Example:

Q1: "How many more people live in Paris's metropolitan area than in the city proper?"

 ●   Steps (the agent plans and picks tools):
      ○ Plan: decompose the two part, needs two figures, then do subtraction.
      ○ Web Search / collection lookup: city proper ≈ 2.1M; metro ≈ 11.3M. The
          current metro area population of Paris in 2025 is 11,347,000.
      ○ Reflect: "Do I have both numbers? Yes." → proceed.
      ○ Calculator tool: 11.3M − 2.1M = 9.2M.

Final answer:
“About 9.2 million more people live in the Paris metropolitan area than in the city proper.”

Chain-of-RAG (CoRAG)
What if my question looks like this?

"What's the nationality of the director of the film that won Best Picture the year Titanic was released?"



The Problem with Traditional RAG:


  ●   Imperfect Retrieval: a single-step retrieval often fails to gather all relevant information for
      complex tasks


  ●   Static Reasoning: the lack of iterative refinement leads to suboptimal generation


  ●   Contextual Misalignment: queries are not adapted dynamically as new information
      surfaces
Chain-of-RAG (CoRAG)
What CoRAG Does Differently?

 ●    CoRAG extends the RAG framework by enabling dynamic, multi-step retrieval.

 ●    Rather than stopping after one lookup, it iteratively refines queries based on intermediate
      states, mirroring how humans approach problem-solving.

 ●    This makes it especially strong at multi-hop question answering.




  ●   Sub-query 1: "When was Titanic released?" → 1997.
  ●   Sub-query 2: "What won Best Picture for 1997 films?" → Titanic itself (at the 1998 ceremony).
  ●   Sub-query 3: "Who directed Titanic?" → James Cameron.
  ●   Sub-query 4: "What is James Cameron's nationality?" → Canadian.
CoRAG: Mechanics
How it works?

 ●   Dynamic Retrieval Chains: starting from the initial query, CoRAG iteratively refines and
     retrieves sub-queries and sub-answers until sufficient information is gathered


 ●   Rejection Sampling for Training: it generates intermediate retrieval chains to augment
     training datasets, improving the model's reasoning capabilities


 ●   Flexible Decoding Strategies: at inference it uses methods like greedy decoding, best-of-N
     sampling, and tree search to balance performance and computational cost

RAG: Assessment Framework
RAG boosts LLMs with external knowledge, but introduces failure points the source article identifies:

  ●    Retrieval issues — wrong documents retrieved or key information missing
  ●    Hallucination risk — the LLM generates content not in the retrieved documents
  ●    Context utilization — the LLM fails to use retrieved information effectively


Without evaluation, these lead to inaccurate or fabricated responses which is critical in high-stakes
domains like pharmaceutical research where accuracy is paramount.


 Two Leading Frameworks

   ●    RAGAS
   ●    TruLens
RAGAS
Retrieval-Augmented Generation Assessment Suite (RAGAS)

 ●   It is an open-source, RAG evaluation framework

 ●   It measures how well a system retrieves and utilises external context to generate accurate,
     faithful and relevant responses.

 ●   RAGAS evaluates the entire RAG pipeline i.e from context retrieval to answer generation


 Answer Relevancy: Measures how closely the generated response matches the user’s query intent.
 Faithfulness: Evaluates whether the response stays true to the retrieved evidence.
 Context Precision: Assesses how relevant the retrieved chunks are to the query.
 Context Recall: Determines whether all necessary information was retrieved.
 Evaluation Dataset: Represents the structured input including questions, contexts, responses and
 reference answers used for metric computation
