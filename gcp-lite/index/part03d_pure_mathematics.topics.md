### Abstract algebra

- **Groups, subgroups and homomorphisms**: group axioms; the subgroup test; cyclic groups and element orders; Lagrange's theorem as |G| = |H|·[G : H]; homomorphisms, kernels and images; the first isomorphism theorem G/ker φ ≅ im φ, and the second and third isomorphism theorems
- **Group actions and the orbit–stabiliser theorem**: an action of G on X as a homomorphism into the symmetric group on X; orbits and stabilisers with |Gx|·|G_x| = |G|; the class equation |G| = |Z(G)| + Σ [G : C_G(xᵢ)] over representatives of the non-central conjugacy classes; Cauchy's theorem that a prime p dividing |G| gives an element of order p
- **Normal subgroups and quotient groups**: normal subgroups as kernels; the quotient G/N with coset multiplication; the correspondence theorem between subgroups of G/N and subgroups of G containing N; the commutator subgroup and the abelianisation G/[G, G]
- **Sylow's theorems**: for |G| = pⁿm with p ∤ m, Sylow p-subgroups of order pⁿ exist (Sylow I, by induction on |G| using the class equation); any two are conjugate (Sylow II); their number n_p divides m and satisfies n_p ≡ 1 (mod p) (Sylow III); a Sylow p-subgroup is normal exactly when n_p = 1; worked use: every group of order 15 is cyclic, and a group of order 60 with more than one Sylow 5-subgroup has exactly six of them and 24 elements of order 5
- **Simple groups, composition series and solvability**: simple groups; composition series and the Jordan–Hölder theorem (the composition factors are unique up to order and isomorphism); solvable groups as those with a series whose factors are abelian; A₅ as the smallest non-abelian simple group, of order 60, shown by a Sylow count
- **The Lorentz group**: the group O(1, 3) of real 4 × 4 matrices Λ with ΛᵀηΛ = η for η = diag(−1, 1, 1, 1), the linear maps that preserve the interval −t² + x² + y² + z²; a closed Lie subgroup of GL(4, ℝ) of dimension 6; it has four connected components distinguished by the sign of det Λ and the sign of the time–time entry Λ⁰₀, and the identity component SO⁺(1, 3) is generated by rotations and boosts, each boost being a one-parameter subgroup along a spatial direction
- **Finite abelian groups: the structure theorem**: every finite abelian group is a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, unique up to the order of the factors (elementary divisors); the invariant-factor form with d₁ | d₂ | … | d_k; counting abelian groups of a given order as a product of partition counts over the prime exponents
- **Rings, ideals and quotient rings**: commutative rings with unity; integral domains and fields; ideals, principal ideals and the ideal generated by a set; quotient rings R/I and the ring isomorphism theorems; prime ideals (R/P a domain) and maximal ideals (R/M a field); the Chinese remainder theorem as R/(I ∩ J) ≅ R/I × R/J when I + J = R
- **Euclidean domains, PIDs and UFDs**: a Euclidean domain has a norm and a division algorithm, and every Euclidean domain is a PID; in a PID every ascending chain of ideals stabilises, irreducible elements are prime, and a PID is a UFD; Z[x] is a UFD but not a PID, since (2, x) is not principal; Gauss's lemma that R a UFD implies R[x] a UFD
- **Polynomial rings and irreducibility**: division algorithm; the factor theorem; content and primitive polynomials; Gauss's lemma transferring factorisation from Z[x] to Q[x]; Eisenstein's criterion; reduction modulo a prime to test irreducibility over Q
- **Modules over a ring**: R-modules as abelian groups with a compatible ring action; submodules, quotient modules and homomorphisms; free modules with bases; the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID, R^r ⊕ R/(d₁) ⊕ … ⊕ R/(d_k) with d₁ | d₂ | … | d_k; the rank and torsion submodule; the Jordan normal form of a matrix over a field as the structure theorem for a vector space viewed as a module over k[x]
- **Field extensions, degree and constructibility**: a field extension K/F and its degree [K : F] as the dimension of K over F; the tower law [L : F] = [L : K]·[K : F]; simple extensions F(α) with [F(α) : F] equal to the degree of the minimal polynomial; algebraic versus transcendental elements; ruler-and-compass constructions as towers of degree two, and the impossibility of doubling the cube and trisecting a general angle
- **Splitting fields and algebraic closure**: splitting fields of a polynomial, their existence and uniqueness up to isomorphism; the bound [K : F] ≤ n!; separable polynomials with distinct roots; perfect fields, including every field of characteristic zero; the algebraic closure, with existence and uniqueness stated
- **Finite fields as extensions**: GF(pⁿ) as the splitting field of x^(pⁿ) − x over F_p; the subfields of GF(pⁿ) correspond to the divisors of n, with GF(p^d) ⊂ GF(pⁿ) exactly when d | n; the construction F_p[x]/(f) for an irreducible f of degree n, with the Frobenius map as the generator of the Galois group
- **Galois extensions, separability and the fundamental theorem**: automorphisms fixing a base field; a finite extension is Galois exactly when it is normal and separable, equivalent to |Gal(K/F)| = [K : F]; the fundamental theorem of Galois theory: a bijection between intermediate fields E and subgroups H of Gal(K/F), inclusion-reversing, with [K : E] = |H| and [E : F] = [G : H]; a Galois extension of a finite field has cyclic Galois group
- **Galois groups of polynomials and cyclotomic extensions**: the Galois group of a polynomial as the permutation group on its roots; irreducible quadratics with group Z/2; the cyclotomic field Q(ζₙ) with Galois group (Z/nZ)ˣ; the Galois group of an irreducible cubic is S₃ exactly when its discriminant is not a square in the base field, and A₃ ≅ ℤ/3 when it is a square
- **Roots of unity as a cyclic group**: the n-th roots of unity μₙ = {z : zⁿ = 1} form a group under multiplication that is cyclic of order n, generated by the primitive root ζₙ = e^(2πi/n); the element ζₙᵏ has order n/gcd(k, n), so the generators are exactly the powers with gcd(k, n) = 1 and there are φ(n) primitive n-th roots; the finite subgroup of roots of unity in any field is cyclic, since every finite subgroup of the multiplicative group of a field is cyclic
- **Solvability by radicals**: a tower of radical extensions; a polynomial is solvable by radicals exactly when its Galois group is a solvable group (Galois's theorem); the general quintic has Galois group S₅, which is not solvable, so no general radical formula exists (the Abel–Ruffini theorem); solvable groups of degree below five
- **Representation theory of finite groups: modules and Maschke's theorem**: a representation as a homomorphism into GL(V); the group algebra and its modules; irreducible representations and Schur's lemma (an intertwiner between irreducibles is zero or an isomorphism; over an algebraically closed field the endomorphisms of an irreducible are scalars); Maschke's theorem that every representation over a field whose characteristic does not divide |G| is completely reducible, proved by averaging a projection over G
- **Characters and orthogonality relations**: the character χ(g) = tr ρ(g); class functions; the inner product ⟨χ, ψ⟩ = (1/|G|) Σ χ(g) conj ψ(g); the first orthogonality relation for irreducible characters; the number of irreducible characters equals the number of conjugacy classes; the column orthogonality relation; the degree identity Σ dᵢ² = |G| from the regular representation
- **Character tables of small groups**: the character tables of the cyclic group, S₃, the dihedral group of order eight and A₄ computed by hand from the orthogonality relations; the degrees of irreducible characters divide |G|; Burnside's theorem that a group of order pᵃqᵇ is solvable, stated with the character-theoretic proof idea

### Point-set and algebraic topology

- **Topological spaces, bases and subbases**: the open-set axioms; neighbourhood bases; a base generating a topology; subbases; subspace topology; the order topology on ℝ, the Sorgenfrey line and the cofinite topology as contrasting examples; finer and coarser topologies
- **Continuity, homeomorphism and topological invariants**: continuity as preimages of open sets equal open; continuity at a point; open and closed maps; homeomorphism as a bijection continuous in both directions; invariants such as connectedness, compactness and the number of path components; the Möbius band (a surface with boundary) and the Klein bottle (closed) as non-homeomorphic surfaces
- **Connectedness and path connectedness**: separations; connected spaces; connected components and path components; continuous images of connected spaces; path-connected implies connected; the topologist's sine curve as connected but not path-connected; the intermediate value theorem as a corollary
- **Compactness and the Tychonoff theorem**: open covers and finite subcovers; closed subsets of compact sets; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed; the finite intersection property; Tychonoff's theorem that an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact, proved with Alexander's subbase lemma or Zorn's lemma; Heine–Borel as the case of ℝⁿ
- **Separation axioms**: T₀, T₁ and T₂ (Hausdorff); regular and normal spaces; with T₁ built into the definitions of normal and regular, the chain normal ⇒ regular ⇒ Hausdorff ⇒ T₁ ⇒ T₀; compact Hausdorff spaces are normal; the cofinite topology as T₁ but not T₂
- **Urysohn's lemma, Tietze extension and metrisation**: in a normal space any two disjoint closed sets are separated by a continuous f : X → [0, 1], built on the dyadic rationals; Tietze's extension theorem for continuous functions from a closed subspace of a normal space; Urysohn's metrisation theorem that a second-countable regular space is metrisable
- **Quotient topology**: the final topology induced by a surjection; the universal property of quotient maps; the circle as an interval with endpoints identified; the real projective plane as a quotient of the sphere by antipodal identification; attaching cells to build spaces
- **Product topology and its universal property**: the product topology generated by cylinder sets; projections as open continuous maps; a map into a product is continuous exactly when each coordinate is; countable products and metrisability; the box topology as strictly finer than the product topology on infinite products
- **The fundamental group**: path homotopy; the group π₁(X, x₀) of loops up to path homotopy, with concatenation; the group axioms proved; induced homomorphisms and homotopy invariance; π₁ of the circle is ℤ by path lifting to the universal cover (proof sketch); the spheres Sⁿ for n ≥ 2 are simply connected; the Brouwer fixed-point theorem for the disc in dimension two from the non-triviality of π₁(S¹)
- **Covering spaces and the lifting criteria**: covering maps with evenly covered neighbourhoods; unique path lifting and homotopy lifting; the lifting criterion for maps from a path-connected space into a base with a covering; the universal cover; π₁(X) as the deck group of a universal covering, and the classification of covers of a nice space by subgroups of π₁ (statement)
- **Van Kampen's theorem**: for open sets U and V covering a path-connected space with path-connected intersection, π₁(X) is the amalgamated free product π₁(U) ∗ over π₁(U ∩ V) of π₁(V); applications: the free group on the circles of a wedge of circles, ℤ² for the torus, and the presentation of the fundamental group of a genus-g surface
- **Simplicial complexes and simplicial homology**: abstract and geometric simplicial complexes; k-simplices, faces and orientation; chain groups Cₖ with boundary ∂ₖ and ∂ₖ₋₁ ∘ ∂ₖ = 0; homology Hₖ = ker ∂ₖ / im ∂ₖ₊₁; Betti numbers as ranks of the free parts; the boundary of a tetrahedron with H₀ = ℤ and H₂ = ℤ, and a seven-vertex triangulation of the torus with Betti numbers 1, 2, 1
- **Smith normal form and torsion in homology**: integer matrices reduced by row and column operations to a diagonal matrix with dᵢ | dᵢ₊₁; the invariant factors read off the torsion subgroup of homology and the free rank read off the nonzero count of the diagonal; the worked boundary matrices of a small complex
- **Singular homology and homotopy invariance**: singular simplices; the singular chain complex; ∂² = 0 proved by cancellation; chain homotopy as the mechanism giving homotopy invariance; the Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence for an open cover, with its connecting homomorphism; H₀ as the free abelian group on path components; Hₖ(Sⁿ) equal to ℤ for k = 0 and k = n and zero otherwise
- **Euler characteristic and the Euler–Poincaré formula**: χ = Σ (−1)ᵏ fₖ over cells equals Σ (−1)ᵏ rank Hₖ over the rationals for finite complexes; invariance under subdivision; χ(S²) = 2, χ(torus) = 0 and χ(real projective plane) = 1; the classification of closed surfaces by orientability and Euler characteristic (statement)

### Pure complex analysis

- **Cauchy–Goursat theorem and the Cauchy integral formula**: Goursat's proof of Cauchy's theorem without assuming f′ continuous; primitives on discs; the Cauchy integral formula proved by shrinking a circle; holomorphic functions are infinitely differentiable; Morera's theorem as the converse of Cauchy's theorem; the Cauchy estimates |f⁽ⁿ⁾(z₀)| ≤ n!·M / r^n
- **Taylor and Laurent expansions with convergence regions**: the Taylor series converging in the largest disc around the centre that avoids singularities; the Laurent series on an annulus with coefficients given by contour integrals; Riemann's removable singularity theorem; the Casorati–Weierstrass theorem for essential singularities; orders of zeros and poles
- **The argument principle**: for a meromorphic f with no zeros or poles on a contour γ, (1/2πi)∮ f′/f = Z − P counting zeros and poles inside; the interpretation as the winding number of f∘γ about zero
- **Rouché's theorem**: if |g| < |f| on a contour γ, then f and f + g have the same number of zeros inside γ; the use for counting roots of polynomials in a disc, with the fundamental theorem of algebra as a corollary
- **Liouville's theorem and the fundamental theorem of algebra**: a bounded entire function is constant, by the Cauchy estimates; a non-constant polynomial has a root, by applying Liouville's theorem to 1/p(z); the Picard little theorem stated (a non-constant entire function omits at most one complex value)
- **Maximum modulus, Schwarz lemma and open mapping**: a non-constant holomorphic function has no interior maximum of |f|; the minimum modulus principle where f has no zeros; the Schwarz lemma for f : D → D with f(0) = 0, giving |f(z)| ≤ |z| and |f′(0)| ≤ 1 with equality exactly for rotations; the open mapping theorem for non-constant holomorphic functions; the identity theorem for functions agreeing on a set with an accumulation point
- **Möbius transformations and the cross-ratio**: maps z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d) with ad − bc ≠ 0 acting on the Riemann sphere; the group PSL(2, ℂ); the three-point transitivity of Möbius maps; the cross-ratio as the invariant of four points; classification of Möbius maps by fixed points and trace; the disc and the upper half-plane as Möbius-equivalent
- **The Riemann mapping theorem**: every simply connected proper open subset of ℂ is conformally equivalent to the unit disc; the proof structure: normal families and Montel's theorem, the extremal problem of maximising |f′(0)| over univalent maps into the disc, and Hurwitz's theorem showing the extremal map is surjective; the Schwarz–Christoffel formula for mapping the disc onto a polygon as a worked example
- **Analytic continuation and the monodromy theorem**: continuation of a power series past its disc by overlapping discs; uniqueness of continuation along paths; the monodromy theorem on simply connected domains; the Riemann surfaces of log z and √z as branched covers
- **Harmonic functions and the Dirichlet problem on the disc**: the real and imaginary parts of holomorphic functions as harmonic; the mean value property; the Poisson integral formula solving the Dirichlet problem on the disc; Harnack's inequality (statement)

### Measure theory and functional analysis

- **σ-algebras and Borel sets**: σ-algebras and the σ-algebra generated by a family; the Borel σ-algebra of ℝⁿ generated by open sets; measurable spaces and measurable functions defined by preimages; measurability preserved under sums, products, limits and composition with continuous maps
- **Outer measure and Carathéodory's theorem**: an outer measure as a monotone, countably subadditive set function vanishing on the empty set; Carathéodory measurability; the theorem that the measurable sets form a σ-algebra on which the outer measure is countably additive; Lebesgue outer measure on ℝⁿ as the infimum of box covers
- **Lebesgue measure regularity and non-measurable sets**: Lebesgue measure as the Carathéodory measure of Lebesgue outer measure; translation invariance; regularity as approximation from inside by compact sets and from outside by open sets; the Cantor set with measure zero and cardinality of the continuum; Vitali's non-measurable set, which uses the axiom of choice
- **Radon–Nikodym theorem and the Lebesgue decomposition**: absolute continuity ν ≪ μ; for σ-finite measures, dν = f dμ with f = dν/dμ; proof sketch via the Hilbert space argument; the Lebesgue decomposition of ν into an absolutely continuous part and a singular part
- **Lebesgue integral and the convergence theorems**: the integral of simple functions; the integral of nonnegative measurable functions as a supremum; the monotone convergence theorem; the dominated convergence theorem with |fₙ| ≤ g integrable; Fatou's lemma ∫ liminf fₙ ≤ liminf ∫ fₙ, with the strict-inequality example of sliding indicator bumps; agreement with the improper Riemann integral for absolutely integrable functions
- **Product measures and Fubini–Tonelli**: the product σ-algebra and product measure on σ-finite spaces; Tonelli's theorem for nonnegative measurable functions, where iterated integrals in either order agree; Fubini's theorem for integrable functions; the counterexample f(x, y) = (x² − y²)/(x² + y²)² on [0, 1]², whose iterated integrals differ, showing that integrability is needed; the scaling of Lebesgue measure by |det| under linear maps
- **Lᵖ spaces, Hölder and Minkowski inequalities**: Lᵖ(μ) for 1 ≤ p < ∞ as classes of measurable functions with finite p-norm, identified almost everywhere; Hölder's inequality ∫|fg| ≤ ‖f‖ₚ‖g‖_q with 1/p + 1/q = 1, derived from Young's inequality; Minkowski's inequality as the triangle inequality ‖f + g‖ₚ ≤ ‖f‖ₚ + ‖g‖ₚ; the Riesz–Fischer theorem that Lᵖ is complete; density of simple functions and of continuous compactly supported functions in Lᵖ
- **Banach spaces and their basic examples**: normed spaces; Banach spaces as complete normed spaces; ℓᵖ, C[a, b] with the supremum norm and Lᵖ as Banach spaces; the criterion that absolutely convergent series converge in a normed space exactly when the space is complete; bounded linear operators and their operator norm, with the space of bounded operators itself Banach
- **Compactness criterion for finite dimensionality (Riesz)**: a normed space is finite-dimensional exactly when its closed unit ball is compact; in a finite-dimensional space all norms are equivalent and the ball is compact by Heine–Borel; conversely, if the unit ball is compact, Riesz's lemma (for a closed proper subspace Y and 0 < θ < 1 there is x with ‖x‖ = 1 and dist(x, Y) ≥ θ) produces a sequence in the ball with no convergent subsequence, unless the space is finite-dimensional
- **Dual spaces and the Riesz representation theorem for Lᵖ**: the dual of Lᵖ for 1 < p < ∞ identified with L^q through integration against g; the dual of L¹ identified with L^∞ on σ-finite spaces; L^∞ having a dual strictly larger than L¹; the Riesz–Markov theorem identifying the dual of C₀(X) with regular Borel measures (statement); the Riesz theorem for Hilbert space duals as the special case p = 2
- **Baire category and the uniform boundedness principle**: the Baire category theorem for complete metric spaces; the Banach–Steinhaus theorem that a pointwise bounded family of bounded operators on a Banach space is uniformly bounded in operator norm; an application as a divergence result for Fourier series
- **Open mapping and closed graph theorems**: the open mapping theorem, a surjective bounded operator between Banach spaces maps open sets to open sets, so its inverse is bounded; the closed graph theorem, a closed linear operator defined on all of a Banach space is bounded; the bounded inverse theorem as the corollary
- **Weak and weak-* topologies**: weak convergence of sequences; the weak topology on a normed space; the weak-* topology on a dual space; the Banach–Alaoglu theorem that closed balls of dual spaces are weak-* compact (statement and sketch); reflexive spaces, with bounded sets weakly sequentially compact in Hilbert spaces and in Lᵖ for 1 < p < ∞
- **Compact operators and the spectrum of bounded operators**: the adjoint T*; the spectrum σ(T) and the resolvent set; compact operators as norm limits of finite-rank operators; the Fredholm alternative for I − K with K compact (injective exactly when surjective); the spectrum of a compact operator consisting of 0 and eigenvalues accumulating only at 0; the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators is taught in part 03b

### Differential geometry

- **Parametrised curves, arc length and reparametrisation**: regular curves with γ′ ≠ 0; arc length s(t) = ∫|γ′(u)| du; arc-length parametrisation; invariance of length under reparametrisation; the unit tangent T
- **Curvature and torsion of space curves**: κ = ‖γ″‖ for arc-length parametrisation; the principal normal N, the binormal B = T × N, and the torsion τ = −N · B′; the planar curvature of a graph κ = |f″| / (1 + f′²)^(3/2); the fundamental theorem of local curve theory: curvature and torsion determine a curve up to a rigid motion
- **Frenet–Serret formulas**: T′ = κN, N′ = −κT + τB, B′ = −τN, written as a matrix equation with a skew-symmetric coefficient matrix; the proof from orthonormality of the frame; the helix with constant κ and τ computed explicitly, and the circle as the case τ = 0
- **Regular surfaces and parametrisations**: a regular surface as the image of a patch with injective differential; charts and transition maps; the tangent plane spanned by x_u and x_v; the unit normal N = (x_u × x_v) / ‖x_u × x_v‖ locally; the sphere, cylinder and torus; orientability via a global unit normal and the Möbius band as non-orientable
- **First fundamental form and intrinsic geometry**: I = E du² + 2F du dv + G dv² with E = ⟨x_u, x_u⟩, F = ⟨x_u, x_v⟩ and G = ⟨x_v, x_v⟩; arc length on the surface; the area element √(EG − F²) du dv; isometries preserve the first fundamental form; the cylinder as locally isometric to the plane
- **Second fundamental form and principal curvatures**: II = e du² + 2f du dv + g dv² with e = ⟨x_uu, N⟩, f = ⟨x_uv, N⟩ and g = ⟨x_vv, N⟩; the Weingarten map (shape operator) S = −dN; principal curvatures as the eigenvalues of S; Gaussian curvature K = det S = (eg − f²) / (EG − F²); mean curvature H = ½ tr S = (eG − 2fF + gE) / (2(EG − F²))
- **Gauss's Theorema Egregium**: the Gaussian curvature depends only on the first fundamental form; proof from the Gauss formulas expressing second derivatives of x in the tangent and normal directions with Christoffel symbols, and the Codazzi–Mainardi equations; the consequence that a sphere cannot be mapped isometrically onto a plane, while a cylinder can be unrolled onto one
- **Geodesics and parallel transport**: geodesics as curves with zero geodesic curvature; the geodesic equations with Christoffel symbols; existence and uniqueness of geodesics from a point and a tangent direction by ODE theory; great circles as geodesics of the sphere; Clairaut's relation for surfaces of revolution; parallel transport along a curve and holonomy around a closed loop
- **Gauss–Bonnet theorem**: the local form ∬ K dA + ∮ k_g ds + Σ exterior angles = 2π for a simple geodesic-polygon region; the global form ∬_S K dA = 2π χ(S) for a closed orientable surface; the spherical triangle's angle excess equal to its area divided by R²; the link to the Euler characteristic of group 2
- **Constant-curvature geometries**: the sphere (K = 1/R²) with angle sums above π; the Euclidean plane (K = 0); the hyperbolic plane (K = −1) with the Poincaré disc metric 4|dz|² / (1 − |z|²)²; the failure of Euclid's parallel postulate in the spherical and hyperbolic cases, with the triangle angle sum derived from Gauss–Bonnet

### Mathematical logic and axiomatic set theory

- **Propositional logic: compactness and Lindenbaum's lemma**: the syntax of well-formed formulas; valuations and truth tables; tautological consequence; soundness and completeness for propositional logic through a maximal consistent extension (Lindenbaum's lemma); the propositional compactness theorem: a set of formulas is satisfiable exactly when every finite subset is, proved by Tychonoff's theorem on the space of valuations or by König's lemma
- **First-order syntax: terms, formulas and structures**: signatures; terms and formulas; free and bound variables; structures and the Tarski satisfaction relation, defined by recursion on formulas; the substitution lemma; validity and logical consequence; elementary equivalence of structures
- **Proof systems and the soundness theorem**: a Hilbert-style calculus with axioms, modus ponens and generalisation; the soundness theorem, that Γ ⊢ φ implies Γ ⊨ φ, by induction on the length of derivations; the deduction theorem for first-order logic with closed hypotheses; consistency and its relation to satisfiability
- **Gödel's completeness theorem**: every consistent theory has a model; Henkin's proof by adding witnesses for existential formulas, extending to a maximal consistent set (Lindenbaum via Zorn's lemma), and building the canonical model from constants; the corollary Γ ⊨ φ exactly when Γ ⊢ φ; first-order validity is enumerable but not decidable
- **Compactness and the Löwenheim–Skolem theorems**: the first-order compactness theorem, a theory is satisfiable exactly when every finite subset is; the consequence that no theory with arbitrarily large finite models has only finite models; the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem for a countable language (a countable model of any satisfiable theory) and the upward theorem (models of every infinite cardinality at least the language size); non-standard models of arithmetic
- **Arithmetisation of syntax and representability**: Gödel numbering that codes formulas and proofs as natural numbers; primitive recursive proof predicates; representability of recursive functions in Robinson's arithmetic Q; the diagonal (fixed-point) lemma: for any formula ψ(x) there is a sentence φ with Q ⊢ φ ↔ ψ(⌜φ⌝)
- **Gödel's incompleteness theorems**: the first theorem: a consistent, recursively axiomatised theory extending arithmetic is incomplete, proved with the diagonal lemma applied to the provability predicate, giving a Gödel sentence G true but unprovable; the second theorem: such a theory does not prove its own consistency Con(T), by the Hilbert–Bernays–Löb derivability conditions; Rosser's strengthening of the first theorem; the limits they place on Hilbert's programme
- **The ZFC axioms and their roles**: extensionality, empty set, pairing, union, power set, infinity, replacement, separation as a schema, and foundation (regularity); the construction of the natural numbers, ordered pairs and functions inside ZFC; the cumulative hierarchy Vα as the intended universe; the ordered pair (a, b) = {{a}, {a, b}} (Kuratowski's definition), whose characteristic property is that (a, b) = (c, d) if and only if a = c and b = d, so that the pair, unlike the set {a, b}, records which element comes first
- **The axiom of choice and Zorn's lemma**: the axiom of choice for families of nonempty sets; equivalents: Zorn's lemma (a partial order in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element), the well-ordering theorem and Tychonoff's theorem; the Banach–Tarski paradox as a consequence (statement); the independence of choice from ZF, by Gödel's constructible universe and Cohen's forcing (statement)
- **Ordinals and transfinite induction**: von Neumann ordinals as transitive sets well-ordered by membership; the ordinal ω, successor and limit ordinals; ordinal addition and multiplication, noncommutative in general; transfinite induction and transfinite recursion; the Burali-Forti paradox, which shows that the ordinals do not form a set
- **Cardinals, Cantor's theorem and the continuum hypothesis**: cardinals as initial ordinals; |ℕ| = ℵ₀; Cantor's theorem |P(X)| > |X| by the diagonal argument; κ + λ = κ · λ = max(κ, λ) for infinite cardinals with a nonzero term; 2^ℵ₀ as the continuum; the continuum hypothesis ℵ₁ = 2^ℵ₀ independent of ZFC, by Gödel's consistency proof and Cohen's independence proof (statement)

### Pure combinatorics and graph theory

- **Ordinary generating functions and formal power series**: the ring of formal power series; the generating function A(x) = Σ aₙxⁿ and products as convolutions; the Catalan generating function C(x) = (1 − √(1 − 4x)) / (2x) and its quadratic equation; extraction of coefficients from rational generating functions by partial fractions; exponential generating functions Σ aₙxⁿ / n! for labelled structures
- **Asymptotic enumeration and singularity analysis**: the growth rate of coefficients set by the dominant singularity of the generating function; the transfer theorem (statement); the Catalan growth 4ⁿ / (n^(3/2) √π) derived from the singularity at x = 1/4; Stirling's approximation n! ~ √(2πn)(n / e)ⁿ as a check
- **Burnside's lemma and Pólya counting**: the number of orbits of G acting on X equals (1/|G|) Σ_g |Fix(g)|, proved by double counting orbit–stabiliser pairs; necklace counting under the cyclic group; the cycle index and Pólya's theorem for counting colourings up to symmetry
- **Ramsey's theorem and Ramsey numbers**: R(s, t) as the least n such that every 2-colouring of the edges of Kₙ contains a red Kₛ or a blue Kₜ; finiteness by induction with R(s, t) ≤ R(s − 1, t) + R(s, t − 1); the Erdős–Szekeres theorem on monotone subsequences as a special case; the exact values R(3, 3) = 6 and R(4, 4) = 18
- **The probabilistic method and Lovász local lemma**: the first-moment method, giving the lower bound R(k, k) > 2^(k/2) by a random 2-colouring; the alteration method; the Lovász local lemma (statement): if each bad event has probability at most p and depends on at most d others with e·p·(d + 1) ≤ 1, then with positive probability none occurs
- **Turán's theorem and extremal graphs**: the maximum number of edges in a graph on n vertices with no K_(r+1) is attained by the Turán graph T(n, r), the balanced complete r-partite graph; the proof by Zykov symmetrisation; the edge count (1 − 1/r)·n²/2 asymptotically
- **Erdős–Stone theorem**: ex(n, H) = (1 − 1/(χ(H) − 1) + o(1)) · n²/2 for every graph H with chromatic number at least three; the role of the chromatic number as the extremal parameter; the Kővári–Sós–Turán bound for bipartite H as the case where the density tends to zero
- **Hall's marriage theorem**: a bipartite graph has a matching saturating one side exactly when |N(S)| ≥ |S| for every subset S of that side; proof by induction on |S|; the deficiency form; systems of distinct representatives as the application
- **König's theorem and the duality of matchings**: in a bipartite graph the maximum matching size equals the minimum vertex cover size, equivalent to max-flow min-cut; augmenting paths; the Hungarian algorithm for weighted bipartite matching in O(n³); Edmonds' blossom algorithm for maximum matchings in general graphs
- **Tutte's theorem on perfect matchings**: a graph has a perfect matching exactly when, for every vertex set S, the number of odd components of G − S is at most |S|; proof sketch by reduction to Hall's theorem; the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition as a statement
- **Planarity and Euler's formula**: a planar graph drawn without crossings; Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 for connected plane graphs, proved by induction on edges; the bounds E ≤ 3V − 6 for simple planar graphs with V ≥ 3 and E ≤ 2V − 4 for triangle-free ones; K₅ and K₃,₃ as non-planar from these bounds
- **Kuratowski's and Wagner's characterisations**: a graph is planar exactly when it has no subgraph that is a subdivision of K₅ or K₃,₃ (statement and the forward direction sketched); Wagner's minor version; linear-time planarity testing by the Hopcroft–Tarjan algorithm
- **Vertex colourings, Brooks' theorem and greedy bounds**: proper colourings; χ(G) ≥ ω(G) (clique number); greedy colouring gives χ ≤ Δ + 1; Brooks' theorem that χ(G) ≤ Δ(G) unless G is a complete graph or an odd cycle, proved by induction with the degree argument; degeneracy-based colouring bounds
- **Edge colourings and Vizing's theorem**: the chromatic index χ′(G) ∈ {Δ, Δ + 1} by Vizing's theorem, proved with maximal fans and Kempe chains; class 1 and class 2 graphs; K_n for odd n as class 2; NP-completeness of deciding class 1 (statement)
- **The four-colour theorem as a statement**: every planar graph has a proper vertex colouring with four colours; the reduction to a finite set of unavoidable configurations, checked by computer (the 1976 proof), and a later computer-verified proof of the same statement (1997); the five-colour theorem with a Kempe-chain proof as the weaker result proved in full
- **Block designs and Fisher's inequality**: a balanced incomplete block design (v, k, λ) with b blocks, replication r and each pair of points in λ blocks; the relations bk = vr and λ(v − 1) = r(k − 1); Fisher's inequality b ≥ v for non-trivial designs, proved by the rank of the incidence matrix N Nᵀ; the Bruck–Ryser–Chowla condition (statement); the Fano plane (7, 3, 1) as the worked example

### Rigorous partial differential equations

- **Classification of second-order linear PDEs**: the general form Σ aᵢⱼ uᵢⱼ + lower-order terms; the principal symbol matrix A and its eigenvalues; elliptic (A definite), parabolic (one zero eigenvalue) and hyperbolic (one eigenvalue of the opposite sign); the characteristic curves from a(dy)² − 2b dx dy + c(dx)² = 0 in two variables; invariance of the type under change of variables
- **Characteristics and the Cauchy problem**: characteristic surfaces for second-order equations; non-characteristic Cauchy data; d'Alembert's solution of the wave equation and its domain of dependence; Hadamard's ill-posedness of the Cauchy problem for elliptic equations with data on a line
- **The Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem**: for analytic coefficients and analytic non-characteristic Cauchy data, a unique local analytic solution exists (proof by majorant series sketched); Lewy's example of an equation with smooth data and no solution, showing that analyticity cannot be dropped
- **Energy methods and uniqueness**: multiplying by u_t and integrating to obtain the energy identity for the wave equation; uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian from Green's first identity, ∫|∇u|² = 0 on a bounded domain; uniqueness and L² stability for the heat equation with Gronwall's inequality
- **Weak derivatives and Sobolev spaces**: the weak derivative defined by integration against test functions in C_c^∞; the Sobolev space Wᵏ,ᵖ(Ω) with the norm summing Lᵖ norms of derivatives up to order k; Hᵏ = Wᵏ,² as a Hilbert space; density of smooth functions; the Sobolev embedding W¹,ᵖ ⊂ L^(p*) with 1/p* = 1/p − 1/n for p < n; Morrey's embedding into Hölder continuous functions for p > n; the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem that H¹ embeds compactly in L² on bounded domains
- **Weak solutions and the Lax–Milgram theorem**: the weak formulation a(u, v) = ℓ(v) for all v in H¹₀; boundedness and coercivity of a give a unique solution, by the Riesz representation theorem applied to the equivalent inner product; the Poincaré inequality on bounded domains supplying coercivity of the Dirichlet form
- **Elliptic regularity and the Dirichlet problem for Poisson's equation**: the weak solution of −Δu = f on a bounded domain with u = 0 on the boundary, unique in H¹₀; regularity by which f ∈ L² gives u ∈ H²; bootstrapping to higher smoothness; the discrete spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian with eigenvalues tending to infinity (statement)
- **Maximum principles for elliptic and parabolic equations**: for Lu = Σ aᵢⱼ uᵢⱼ + Σ bᵢ uᵢ with Lu ≥ 0, the weak maximum principle, with the maximum over the closure attained on the boundary; Hopf's strong maximum principle for connected domains; the comparison principle; the uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem and the mean value property of harmonic functions as consequences; the parabolic maximum principle for the heat equation
- **Green's functions and fundamental solutions of the Laplacian**: the fundamental solution Φ(x) = −(1/2π) log|x| in two dimensions and Φ(x) = |x|^(2−n) / ((n − 2)ωₙ₋₁) for n ≥ 3, with ωₙ₋₁ the surface area of the unit sphere, satisfying −ΔΦ = δ in the distributional sense; the Dirichlet Green's function G(x, y) on a domain and Green's representation formula for the solution; the Poisson kernel of the disc as the Green's function's boundary derivative; symmetry and positivity of G
- **Heat kernel and Duhamel's principle**: the heat kernel Γ(x, t) = (4πt)^(−n/2) e^(−|x|²/(4t)) as the fundamental solution of u_t = Δu, with the solution on ℝⁿ given by convolution with the initial data; the semigroup property; Duhamel's principle giving the solution of inhomogeneous heat equations as a time-integral of heat-kernel solutions
