Domain: Signals, Systems & Communications

### Signals and linear time-invariant systems

- **LTI systems and impulse response**: linearity and time invariance; the impulse response h[n] as the output to a unit impulse; the output to any input as convolution with h, derived from the sifting property; discrete-time systems given by difference equations; causality and memory read from h; the unit step u[n] as the running sum of the impulse and the step response as the convolution of u with h; convolution's commutativity, associativity and distributivity over addition, which justify reordering cascaded systems and summing parallel ones; the unit impulse δ[n], equal to 1 at n = 0 and 0 elsewhere, with the sifting property Σ_k x[k]·δ[n − k] = x[n], and the unit step u[n], equal to 1 for n ≥ 0 and 0 for n < 0, which is the running sum of δ with δ[n] = u[n] − u[n − 1]; the real exponential x[n] = aⁿ·u[n], whose response through an LTI system with h[n] = bⁿ·u[n] is Σ_(k=0)^n a^k b^(n−k) = (a^(n+1) − b^(n+1)) / (a − b) for a ≠ b, and (n + 1)·aⁿ for a = b; in continuous time the output is the convolution integral y(t) = ∫ x(τ)·h(t − τ) dτ, and h(t) is the output to the Dirac impulse δ(t), with ∫ x(τ)·δ(t − τ) dτ = x(t) as the sifting property
- **Autocorrelation**: r_xx[k] = Σ x[n]·x[n+k] for deterministic and random signals; symmetry and the maximum at zero lag; autocorrelation of an LTI output as the input's autocorrelation filtered by h; the Wiener–Khinchin relation to the power spectral density; biased and unbiased estimators from a finite record; white noise with a flat power spectral density, and the power in a frequency band as the integral of the PSD over that band; cross-correlation r_xy[k] = Σ_n x[n]·y[n + k] between two signals, and the periodic correlation of a period-N sequence, Σ from n = 0 to N − 1 of x[n]·x[(n + k) mod N], which is itself periodic in k with period N
- **Periodic sampling and aliasing**: the sampled spectrum as replicas of the original at multiples of the sampling rate; the Nyquist–Shannon condition and folding when it fails; reconstruction by ideal sinc interpolation; anti-aliasing filters before the converter; a tone above half the sampling rate folding to a lower frequency; bandpass sampling of a signal confined to [f_L, f_H] with bandwidth B = f_H − f_L, where a rate fs is valid when 2f_H/m ≤ fs ≤ 2f_L/(m − 1) for some integer m with 1 ≤ m ≤ ⌊f_H/B⌋ (m = 1 being the lowpass case fs ≥ 2f_H), so the band from 10 to 12 MHz can be sampled at 4 MHz; oversampling at fs = M·2B, where the in-band quantization noise falls by M and the signal-to-quantization-noise ratio gains 10 log₁₀ M dB, half a bit per doubling of M; the A/D converter as a sample-and-hold followed by a quantizer, and the D/A converter as a zero-order hold followed by an analogue reconstruction filter
- **Quadrature signals and the Hilbert transform**: the analytic signal x + j·H{x} and its one-sided spectrum; in-phase and quadrature (I/Q) decomposition of a bandpass signal; instantaneous amplitude and phase read from the analytic signal; why complex baseband mixing underlies every quadrature receiver; the discrete-time Hilbert transform with frequency response −j·sgn(ω) for |ω| < π and impulse response h[n] = 2/(πn) for odd n and 0 for even n, applied to a real sequence to form its discrete analytic signal; the complex baseband (lowpass) equivalent u(t) of a bandpass signal s(t) = Re{u(t)·e^(j2πf_c t)}, where u(t) = s_I(t) + j·s_Q(t) is lowpass, so filtering and mixing are carried out on u(t)
- **BIBO stability**: bounded input gives bounded output exactly when Σ|h[n]| is finite (the integral of |h(t)| in continuous time); the proof by the triangle inequality and a counterexample with a non-summable impulse response
- **Signal classes, energy and power**: energy E = Σ|x[n]|² (or ∫|x(t)|² dt) and power P = lim (1/(2N + 1))·Σ from n = −N to N of |x[n]|², with energy signals having finite E and zero P and power signals having finite nonzero P; system classes memoryless, causal, linear, time-invariant, stable and invertible, each decided from the defining input–output rule; signal-to-noise ratio as the ratio of signal power to noise power, SNR = P_signal ÷ P_noise, in decibels 10 log₁₀(P_signal ÷ P_noise), with the power taken from the definition above; further signal classes: continuous- versus discrete-time signals, continuous- versus discrete-valued signals (classified by amplitude), periodic versus aperiodic, deterministic versus random, single-channel versus multichannel, and one-dimensional versus multidimensional (an image or field over two or more coordinates)
- **Complex exponentials, eigenfunctions and frequency response**: a complex exponential e^(jωn) passed through an LTI system with impulse response h gives H(e^(jω))·e^(jωn), where H(e^(jω)) = Σ h[n]·e^(−jωn) is the frequency response; the magnitude |H| and the phase arg H give the gain and phase shift at each frequency; the phasor form of a real sinusoid, A cos(ωn + φ) = Re{A e^(jφ) e^(jωn)}, in which the complex amplitude A e^(jφ) carries the amplitude and phase, so that sinusoids of one frequency add and shift as phasors
- **Karhunen–Loève expansion**: a zero-mean random process x(t) on [0, T] with covariance kernel K(t, s) = E[x(t)x(s)] is written x(t) = Σ_k X_k·φ_k(t), where the φ_k are orthonormal eigenfunctions of K, ∫K(t, s)φ_k(s) ds = λ_k·φ_k(t), and the coefficients X_k = ∫x(t)φ_k(t) dt are uncorrelated with E[X_k X_j] = λ_k·δ_kj; truncating after M terms gives the smallest mean-square error for an M-term approximation, equal to Σ_{k>M} λ_k; Mercer's theorem guarantees convergence for a continuous, positive semidefinite kernel; for white noise every eigenvalue equals σ², so any orthonormal basis serves; the discrete form, the eigenvectors of a covariance matrix, is principal component analysis
- **Noise models**: a Gaussian random variable with mean μ and variance σ², and its tail probability written with the Q-function; white noise as an uncorrelated sequence with a flat power spectral density; additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) as the model r = s + n with n zero-mean, Gaussian and white, two-sided power spectral density N₀/2, so its variance in the band |f| ≤ B is N₀B; the uniform rounding error of Δ²/12 as the quantization model, accurate when the signal spans many quantizer steps

- **Signal space and Gram–Schmidt**: a finite set of waveforms s_m(t) is treated as a set of vectors in the space they span, with inner product ⟨f, g⟩ = ∫ f(t) g*(t) dt and norm ‖f‖ = √⟨f, f⟩; Gram–Schmidt builds an orthonormal basis φ_k from the waveforms, with uₖ = s_k − Σ_(j<k) ⟨s_k, φ_j⟩ φ_j and φ_k = uₖ / ‖uₖ‖, skipping any s_k for which uₖ = 0, so the dimension is at most the number of waveforms; each waveform is a coefficient vector in this basis, and the distance between two waveforms equals the Euclidean distance between their coefficient vectors, which the minimum-distance receiver uses; a random process has the same structure through the Karhunen–Loève basis above, and a bandpass signal is handled through its complex baseband equivalent

### Transforms and system analysis

- **Laplace transform and its inverse**: X(s) = ∫ x(t)·e^(−st) dt; the transform of a derivative with its initial condition, so a linear differential equation becomes algebra; the inverse by partial fractions and the Bromwich integral; the region of convergence as the half-plane where the integral exists; the initial- and final-value theorems
- **z-transform, its inverse and region of convergence**: X(z) = Σ x[n]·z^(−n); the shift property as multiplication by z^(−1); the inverse by long division, partial fractions and residues; the region of convergence as an annulus that decides causality and whether the unit circle lies inside it, and so stability; the one-sided z-transform X(z) = Σ from n = 0 of x[n]·z^(−n), whose shift rule x[n − 1] ↔ z⁻¹X(z) + x[−1] carries the initial conditions of a difference equation into the transform; and the inverse by the contour integral x[n] = (1/2πj)∮ X(z)·z^(n−1) dz over a counterclockwise contour inside the region of convergence that encloses the poles, which the residue theorem evaluates as the sum of the residues of X(z)·z^(n−1) at those poles
- **Transfer functions**: H(s) = Y(s)/X(s) with zero initial state; H(z) from a difference equation; gain and phase by evaluation on the imaginary axis or the unit circle; poles and zeros as the features that set the shape of the response; deconvolution as the inverse system, with H⁻¹ recovering the input exactly when H has no zeros on the imaginary axis or unit circle and H⁻¹ is stable, and Wiener deconvolution G = H*·S_xx ÷ (|H|²·S_xx + S_nn) under noise, which trades exact inversion for bounded noise amplification; the minimum-phase case, in which every pole and zero of a rational H lies strictly inside the unit circle (or the open left half-plane for H(s)), so that H⁻¹ is causal and stable; the all-pass factorization H = H_min·H_ap of any rational system with no zeros on the circle, where H_ap has unit magnitude at every frequency and H_min has the smallest phase lag and group delay of all systems with the same magnitude response and the largest share of its energy at the start of the impulse response; a zero outside the unit circle as the reason an exact causal inverse is unstable, repaired by reflecting that zero to its conjugate reciprocal inside the circle; and the real cepstrum recovering the minimum-phase impulse response from the log magnitude by folding the cepstral coefficients; reverberation as the room impulse response h convolved with the source, y = h ∗ x, so that the echoes and the reverberant tail are the later samples of h, and dereverberation as deconvolution by an inverse filter for h, in the Wiener form above when noise is present
- **Pole-zero stability**: a causal LTI system is stable exactly when every pole lies in the open left half-plane (continuous time) or strictly inside the unit circle (discrete time); the Routh–Hurwitz and Jury tests; a pole near the boundary as the source of slow, ringing responses
- **Time constant, natural frequency and resonance**: a first-order system with pole at s = −1/τ decays as e^(−t/τ), where the time constant τ is the time for the response to fall to 1/e of its initial value; a second-order system H(s) = ωₙ² / (s² + 2ζωₙs + ωₙ²) has natural frequency ωₙ and damping ratio ζ, with damped frequency ω_d = ωₙ√(1 − ζ²) and step-response overshoot exp(−πζ / √(1 − ζ²)) for 0 < ζ < 1; for ζ < 1/√2 its magnitude peaks at ω_r = ωₙ√(1 − 2ζ²), with peak gain 1 / (2ζ√(1 − ζ²))
- **State-space analysis of LTI systems**: x′ = Ax + Bu and y = Cx + Du (discrete: x[n+1] = Ax[n] + Bu[n]); the transfer function C(sI − A)⁻¹B + D; the eigenvalues of A as the poles; controllability and observability matrices and their rank tests; different realizations of one transfer function; the realization structures of a rational system function: direct form I and direct form II (the canonical form, with N delay elements for an order-N system), the transposed form, the cascade of second-order sections and the parallel partial-fraction form, all with the same input–output behaviour but different internal state and different sensitivity to coefficient rounding
- **Linear feedback systems**: closed-loop transfer function G/(1 + GK) for negative feedback; the characteristic equation 1 + GK = 0; sensitivity and complementary sensitivity; the Nyquist criterion; gain and phase margins as measures of robustness; feedback gain traded against noise amplification
- **Fourier series and transforms in continuous and discrete time**: the Fourier series of a periodic signal with coefficients c_k = (1/T)∫ x(t)·e^(−j2πkt/T) dt, whose magnitudes |c_k| give the harmonic content; the Fourier transform X(ω) = ∫ x(t)·e^(−jωt) dt and its inverse; the discrete-time Fourier transform X(e^(jω)) = Σ x[n]·e^(−jωn) and the discrete-time Fourier series of a periodic sequence; the convolution theorem, under which convolution in time becomes multiplication of spectra; Parseval's identity for energy; convergence at a jump discontinuity to the midpoint, under Dirichlet's conditions; harmonics: the k-th harmonic of a periodic signal of period T is the component at frequency k/T with coefficient c_k, and the total harmonic distortion of a signal whose fundamental has coefficient c₁ is THD = √(Σ_{k≥2}|c_k|²) ÷ |c₁|; best approximation, under which the truncated Fourier series s_N(t) = Σ_(|k| ≤ N) c_k e^(j2πkt/T) minimizes the mean-square error (1/T)∫|x − s_N|² over all trigonometric polynomials of degree N, with minimum error Σ_(|k| > N)|c_k|², which tends to zero as N grows; Bessel's inequality Σ_k |c_k|² ≤ (1/T)∫|x(t)|² dt for any square-integrable periodic signal, with equality exactly when Parseval's identity holds; the energy density spectrum |X(ω)|² of an energy signal and the power density spectrum of a power signal; the DTFT as the z-transform evaluated on the unit circle, X(e^(jω)) = X(z) at z = e^(jω), valid when the region of convergence contains |z| = 1; and the spectral bandwidth as the width of the band over which the energy density stays within 3 dB of its peak, the half-power band

### Digital filters and multirate processing

- **FIR and IIR design**: windowed-sinc and Parks–McClellan equiripple FIR design with linear phase; IIR design by the bilinear transform from Butterworth and Chebyshev analogue prototypes; the trade of FIR length against IIR order and phase distortion; FIR structures: the direct tapped-delay form with N − 1 delays and N multiplications per output, the symmetric linear-phase form that halves the multiplications for a symmetric impulse response, the cascade of short FIR sections, and the frequency-sampling form built from a comb filter and parallel resonators, compared by multiplications and delays per output; the half-band FIR filter, a lowpass with cutoff at a quarter of the sampling rate (ω_c = π/2) whose impulse response is zero at every nonzero even index, so that alternate taps vanish and about half the multiplications of a general FIR of the same length are saved, with passband and stopband ripple equal; the impulse-invariance IIR design, which samples the analogue impulse response, h[n] = T·h_a(nT), so that each analogue pole s_k maps to z = e^(s_k T) and the frequency response is aliased from the analogue one, which suits lowpass prototypes and not highpass ones
- **Window-method FIR design**: the ideal lowpass impulse response h_d[n] = sin(ω_c n) / (πn), with h_d[0] = ω_c / π, truncated to length N and multiplied by a window w[n] symmetric about the centre for linear phase; the transition width is set by the window's main lobe, roughly 3.1π/N for the Hann window (sidelobe about −31 dB), 3.3π/N for Hamming (about −43 dB) and 5.5π/N for Blackman (about −58 dB), so the window sets the ripple and N sets the transition width; for the Kaiser window, the length needed for an attenuation of A dB is N ≈ (A − 7.95) / (2.285 Δω), with Δω the transition width in radians
- **Analogue prototypes and the bilinear map**: the bilinear transform s = (2/T)(1 − z⁻¹) / (1 + z⁻¹) maps the left half-plane inside the unit circle, so a stable analogue prototype gives a stable digital filter, with frequency warping Ω = (2/T) tan(ω/2) that compresses high frequencies, so the critical frequencies are prewarped before design; a Butterworth prototype of order N has |H(jΩ)|² = 1 / (1 + (Ω/Ω_c)^(2N)), with N poles equally spaced on the circle of radius Ω_c in the left half-plane, and order N ≥ log₁₀[(10^(A_s/10) − 1) / (10^(A_p/10) − 1)] / (2 log₁₀(Ω_s/Ω_p)) for passband ripple A_p and stopband attenuation A_s at the edges Ω_p and Ω_s; a Chebyshev type I prototype has |H(jΩ)|² = 1 / (1 + ε²T_N²(Ω/Ω_c)), with passband ripple ε and N poles on an ellipse at σ_k = −sinh(a) sin θ_k and ω_k = cosh(a) cos θ_k, where a = (1/N) asinh(1/ε) and θ_k = π(2k − 1) / (2N), with order N ≥ arccosh √((10^(A_s/10) − 1) / (10^(A_p/10) − 1)) / arccosh(Ω_s/Ω_p)
- **Quantization and fixed-point effects**: uniform quantizer noise of Δ²/12 and the signal-to-quantization-noise ratio of about 6.02b + 1.76 dB; rounding versus truncation bias; Q-format fixed-point arithmetic with overflow; coefficient quantization moving poles and zeros; limit cycles in recursive filters, sustained oscillations with zero input caused by rounding or overflow in the feedback loop; scaling of internal nodes so that none overflows: the scale factor for a node is the L1 norm of the impulse response from the input to that node, times the largest input, so the node's output stays within the word range
- **Multirate processing**: decimation as anti-alias filtering followed by downsampling; interpolation as zero insertion followed by an image-rejection filter; the noble identities that move filters across rate changers; the spectral scaling by M and by L
- **Polyphase filters and sample-rate conversion**: polyphase decomposition of an FIR filter; efficient decimators and interpolators that compute only the retained outputs; rational resampling by L/M; multiplications per output sample as the cost measure
- **Filter banks and wavelets**: analysis and synthesis banks; perfect-reconstruction conditions for two-channel quadrature mirror and biorthogonal designs; the discrete wavelet transform as a multiresolution decomposition with a scaling function and a wavelet; time–frequency localisation compared with the short-time Fourier transform; separable image transforms: the two-dimensional DFT and DCT computed by a one-dimensional transform along rows and then along columns, the DCT-II basis used in JPEG on 8 × 8 blocks, and the two-dimensional discrete wavelet transform as a row and column filter bank applied at each level, splitting an image into an approximation subband (LL) and horizontal, vertical and diagonal detail subbands (LH, HL, HH)
- **CIC filters**: cascaded integrator–comb decimators with transfer function ((1 − z^(−RM))/(1 − z^(−1)))^N; multiplier-free operation with wrap-around arithmetic; passband droop and its correction by a short FIR; output bit growth of N·log₂(RM) bits
- **Differentiators and integrators**: the ideal differentiator, with frequency response jω for |ω| < π, has impulse response h[n] = cos(πn) / n for n ≠ 0 and h[0] = 0, an antisymmetric response with zero gain at DC, which is truncated and windowed to a finite FIR; the ideal integrator (accumulator) has H(z) = 1 / (1 − z⁻¹), realized by the running sum y[n] = y[n − 1] + x[n], and is the first stage of a CIC decimator
- **Linear interpolation between samples**: the value at a fractional position μ between x[n] and x[n + 1], y = (1 − μ)·x[n] + μ·x[n + 1] for 0 ≤ μ < 1, which is a two-tap FIR filter with one multiplication pair per output; its use as a cheap resampler and fractional-delay element, whose error grows, through a low-pass droop, as the input frequency approaches the sampling rate

### Practical spectral analysis

- **Goertzel and chirp-z transforms**: the Goertzel recursion computing one DFT bin with a second-order filter and one multiplication per sample; the chirp-z transform evaluating the z-transform on an arbitrary arc or grid through Bluestein's identity and a convolution; choosing either over the FFT when few or off-grid bins are needed
- **Parseval and reconstruction checks**: Parseval's energy equality Σ|x[n]|² = (1/N)·Σ|X[k]|² for the DFT; round-trip tests of analysis followed by synthesis as unit checks of a transform implementation; the scaling and leakage mistakes these checks catch
- **DFT properties and roundoff**: a circular shift of x by m samples multiplies the N-point DFT by e^(−j2πkm/N), and for real x the DFT is conjugate-symmetric, X[N − k] = X*[k]; the DFT of the circular convolution of two length-N sequences is the pointwise product of their DFTs, while a linear convolution of lengths L₁ and L₂ needs zero padding to length at least L₁ + L₂ − 1 before the product; and the relative rms roundoff error of a radix-2 FFT grows roughly as √(log₂ N) times the unit roundoff for random data, so the error per bin rises only logarithmically with transform size
- **Signal averaging**: coherent averaging of N time-aligned records improves signal-to-noise ratio by √N when the noise is uncorrelated; time alignment as the precondition; exponential averaging and its effective window; bias introduced by misalignment
- **Cepstral analysis**: the real cepstrum c[n] = IDFT(log|X[k]|), turning multiplication in the spectrum into addition; separating a slowly varying spectral envelope from fine structure by liftering; the quefrency axis
- **Parametric signal modelling (LPC)**: the all-pole autoregressive model x[n] = −Σ a_k·x[n−k] + e[n]; the Levinson–Durbin recursion solving the Yule–Walker equations; the prediction-error filter; all-pole spectrum estimates and model-order choice
- **Frequency translation**: multiplying by a complex exponential shifts the spectrum by its frequency; digital down-conversion with a numerically controlled oscillator and I/Q mixing; the image term from real mixing and its removal by a low-pass filter before decimation; translation by fs/4 without multiplications, because the sequences cos(πn/2) and sin(πn/2) take the values 1, 0, −1, 0 and 0, 1, 0, −1, so the mixer needs only sign changes and zeroed samples
- **Radix-2 FFT and bit reversal**: the decimation-in-time recursion splitting an N-point DFT into DFTs of the even- and odd-indexed samples, each of length N/2, combined with twiddle factors W_N^k = e^(−j2πk/N); bit-reversed input ordering so the butterflies run in place; N log₂ N complex multiplications against N² for the direct sum
- **Leakage, windowing and scalloping**: a tone that does not complete a whole number of cycles in the record leaks into neighbouring bins; a window trades main-lobe width against sidelobe level; the scalloping loss is the dip in a bin's level when the tone falls between bins, about 1.42 dB at the midpoint for the Hann window; zero padding, which extends the record to a longer length M with zeros, interpolates the DFT onto a finer grid of spacing fs/M, so the scalloping dip is sampled more finely, but it adds no new information and cannot separate two tones that the original record length does not resolve
- **Sparse FFT**: for a length-N signal whose DFT has only k nonzero bins, sub-linear algorithms recover those bins from about k log N samples instead of N log N work: frequencies are hashed into roughly k buckets by subsampling and permuting the record, each bucket holds a sum of few tones that a filter can isolate, and a single tone's frequency is read from the phase advance between two time-shifted samples; the method is randomized, relies on the sparsity being known or bounded, and falls back to the ordinary FFT when k is close to N

### Digital communications

- **Modulation: AM, PAM, PSK and QAM**: amplitude modulation as a message times a carrier; pulse amplitude modulation mapping bits to levels; phase-shift keying as points on the unit circle; quadrature amplitude modulation as two PAM streams on cosine and sine carriers; Gray mapping; average symbol energy and bandwidth set by the pulse shape; the Nyquist criterion for zero intersymbol interference and the raised-cosine pulse; continuous-phase modulation (CPM), whose transmitted signal is √(2E/T) cos(2πf_c t + φ(t, a)) with phase φ(t, a) = 2πh Σ_k a_k q(t − kT), where h is the modulation index, q(t) = ∫₀ᵗ g(τ) dτ is the phase pulse with q(∞) = ½, and the phase follows a trellis of finitely many states, so that a Viterbi detector applies; and continuous-phase FSK (CPFSK), the case of a rectangular frequency pulse g, in which the phase is continuous at every symbol boundary
- **Optimum receivers in AWGN**: the matched filter as the receiver that maximizes output signal-to-noise ratio, equal to the correlator; the minimum-distance (maximum-likelihood) decision rule for equiprobable symbols; the Q-function error probability, for example BPSK bit error probability Q(√(2E_b/N₀)); the union bound for M-ary constellations
- **Carrier and symbol synchronization (PLL)**: the phase-locked loop with phase detector, loop filter and voltage-controlled oscillator; the loop-bandwidth trade of tracking speed against noise; the Costas loop for suppressed-carrier signals; the Gardner timing-error detector for symbol timing; acquisition and cycle slips
- **Adaptive equalization**: intersymbol interference from a dispersive channel; the least-mean-squares (LMS) and recursive least-squares (RLS) updates for adaptive linear equalizers; decision-feedback equalizers; training sequences and convergence speed; the band-limited channel model, in which the channel's frequency response is zero outside |f| ≤ W, so symbols can be sent at no more than 2W per second without overlapping in the Nyquist sense, and the symbol-spaced model y_k = Σ_n h_n·a_(k−n) + z_k whose taps h_n are the intersymbol interference that the equalizer must cancel
- **Spread spectrum and OFDM**: direct-sequence spreading with pseudo-noise codes and processing gain; frequency hopping; orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing as an inverse DFT over subcarriers; the cyclic prefix turning linear into circular convolution; a one-tap equalizer per subcarrier
- **MIMO and multiple antennas**: the channel matrix H linking transmit and receive antennas; spatial multiplexing gain min(N_t, N_r); diversity from the Alamouti space–time code; capacity log₂ det(I + (SNR/N_t)·HH*) in bits per second per hertz
- **Fading channels (Rayleigh and Ricean)**: multipath, Doppler spread, coherence time and coherence bandwidth; Rayleigh fading without a line-of-sight path and Ricean fading with its K-factor; average bit-error rate over Rayleigh fading and its diversity slope; the ergodic capacity E[log₂(1 + SNR·|h|²)] of a fading channel, where for Rayleigh fading |h|² is exponentially distributed; the outage capacity, the largest rate R for which P(log₂(1 + SNR·|h|²) < R) ≤ p_out, which applies when the code is too short to average over the fades; and coding over fading with interleaving, which spreads a codeword over independent fades so that its diversity order is achieved
- **Multiuser communications**: multiple access by frequency, time and code (FDMA, TDMA, CDMA); the capacity region of the Gaussian multiple-access channel; successive interference cancellation and the order of decoding
- **Channel models**: the additive white Gaussian noise channel r = s + n as the baseline; the linear time-invariant channel r(t) = (h ∗ s)(t) + n(t), whose impulse response h spreads each pulse; the band-limited channel, which zeroes the spectrum outside a band; the fading channel, in which h is a random, time-varying gain, flat or frequency-selective; and the choice of model as the question each receiver design must answer, namely which of these distortions it must remove

### Exercises

- From scratch: a discrete LTI system from its difference equation, its impulse response and its output by convolution, checked against direct simulation; a tone sampled above and below the Nyquist rate, with the aliased frequency predicted before it is measured
- From scratch: a pole-zero and stability checker for a rational transfer function, with a state-space realization and a step response compared with the predicted pole locations
- From scratch: an FIR filter by windowed sinc and an IIR low-pass by the bilinear transform, both designed by hand, then reproduced with a library design routine and compared on frequency response and fixed-point quantization error
- From scratch: a polyphase decimator and interpolator, checked by a spectrum comparison and a round-trip test; a CIC decimator with its bit-width computed before the run
- From scratch: a Goertzel tone detector and an LPC estimator by the Levinson–Durbin recursion, each compared with a periodogram on the same record
- From scratch: a BPSK and QAM link over an AWGN channel at baseband, measuring bit-error rate across a range of Eb/N0 and plotting it against the theoretical Q-function curve
- From scratch: a PLL carrier-recovery loop that locks to a frequency offset, and an OFDM transmitter and receiver with a cyclic prefix and one-tap equalizer over a Rayleigh fading channel
- From scratch: a radix-2 FFT with bit-reversed ordering, checked against the direct DFT on random records and counted in complex multiplications against N log₂ N, then used to measure the Hann-windowed leakage of a tone placed between two bins
- From scratch: a sparse-FFT recovery of the k nonzero bins of a multi-tone record, checked against the full FFT, with the number of samples read compared with N before the run
- **Reasoning drills**
  - Given an impulse response h[n] = 0.9ⁿ for n ≥ 0 and a bounded input, decide BIBO stability by summing |h[n]|, then predict the peak output before simulating it
  - Given a rational transfer function, find its poles and region of convergence, decide stability from the pole locations, then predict the step response before simulating it
  - Show that quantizing a sine wave to b bits gives a signal-to-quantization-noise ratio near 6.02b + 1.76 dB, then measure it and explain any gap
  - Predict the signal-to-noise gain from coherently averaging 64 time-aligned records of a tone in noise, measure it, then predict what a one-sample misalignment does to the gain
  - For a 16-QAM link at a stated Eb/N₀, estimate the bit-error rate from the AWGN curve, then predict how much a Rayleigh fading channel changes it and why
  - For a bandpass signal occupying 10 to 12 MHz, list the sampling rates below 25 MHz that alias no band component, predict which one is smallest, then check it by folding the band
