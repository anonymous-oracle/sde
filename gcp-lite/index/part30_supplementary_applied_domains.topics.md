### Corporate finance

- **Goals and governance**: profit versus shareholder-wealth maximization; the agency problem; corporate governance; sources of finance (equity, debt, hybrids, retained earnings)
- **Time value of money**: compounding and discounting; annuities and perpetuities; the effective annual rate; valuing bonds (yield to maturity) and stocks (dividend discount, Gordon growth); recurring deposits: the maturity value of equal monthly instalments with simple interest on the running balance, I = P·n(n + 1)/(2·12)·r/100; loan amortization: the equal instalment A = P·i/(1 − (1 + i)^(−n)), the schedule splitting each payment into interest on the outstanding balance and principal repaid, and the outstanding balance after k payments; compound interest A = P(1 + r ÷ m)^(mt) for m compounding periods a year over t years, continuous compounding A = Pe^(rt), and the effective annual rate (1 + r ÷ m)^m − 1, which puts rates with different compounding periods on one scale
- **Capital budgeting**: incremental cash flows, sunk and opportunity costs, depreciation tax shields, working capital; NPV, IRR (and multiple IRRs), MIRR, payback and discounted payback, profitability index; ranking conflicts and capital rationing; sensitivity and scenario analysis
- **Return, risk and portfolio theory**: expected return and variance; correlation and diversification; the efficient frontier; CAPM, beta and the security market line; the Kelly criterion for a bet paying b to one at win probability p: stake f* = (bp − q) ÷ b with q = 1 − p, which maximizes the expected log growth of wealth, and over-betting lowers growth even when the edge is positive
- **Horse-race gambling and side information**: with odds o(x) on each outcome x and a fraction b(x) of wealth bet on it, the growth rate of wealth is W(b, p) = Σₓ p(x) log(b(x)·o(x)), maximized by proportional betting b = p, which gives W* = Σₓ p(x) log o(x) − H(X), so the doubling rate is the expected log odds minus the entropy of the outcome; with side information Y known before the race, the best rate rises by exactly I(X; Y), the mutual information, so information is worth its bits in growth rate
- **Cost of capital**: costs of debt, preferred and equity; WACC and firm value; the after-tax cost of debt r_d(1 − t), the cost of preferred stock D ÷ P₀ for a fixed dividend D and price P₀, and the cost of equity from CAPM (r_f + β(r_m − r_f)) or the Gordon model (D₁ ÷ P₀ + g); WACC = (E ÷ V)r_E + (D ÷ V)r_d(1 − t) with V = D + E, the return a project must earn to leave firm value unchanged; firm value as the present value of free cash flows discounted at WACC, which holds only for projects with the firm's own risk
- **Leverage and capital structure**: operating, financial and combined leverage; EBIT–EPS analysis; Modigliani–Miller with and without taxes, trade-off theory, pecking order
- **Working capital**: liquidity measures; operating and cash-conversion cycles; inventory control (EOQ, reorder point); receivables and credit policy; cash management
- **Dividend decisions**: Gordon, Walter and Modigliani–Miller irrelevance; Lintner's smoothing; buybacks and signalling; share arithmetic: shares bought as investment ÷ market price, dividend as shares × face value × dividend rate, and dividend yield as dividend per share ÷ market price
- **Indirect taxes: VAT and GST**: a value-added tax charged at each stage on the value added, so the final consumer bears the whole tax; GST as India's destination-based value-added tax: CGST and SGST split equally on an intra-state supply, IGST on an inter-state supply; input tax credit (ITC) offsetting tax paid on inputs against tax due on output, so net tax = output tax − input tax; computing the tax at each stage of a supply chain and the tax a dealer pays to the government; sales tax as a single-stage tax levied once at the retail sale and collected by the seller, with no input credit, so a tax charged again on each intermediate sale compounds along the chain, which the value-added design avoids
- **Budgeting and savings**: the 50/30/20 split of take-home pay into needs, wants and savings as a starting rule; an emergency fund of three to six months of expenses; the savings rate as saved ÷ income; a sinking fund for a known outlay F due in n periods at rate i, with the level deposit P = F·i ÷ ((1 + i)^n − 1), and the future value of a regular deposit A as A·((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i; the budget as a plan of flows over a period, distinct from the balance sheet as a snapshot of stocks
- **Income tax and credit**: progressive slabs in which each band of taxable income is taxed at its own rate, so the tax is the sum of the band amounts rather than the top rate applied to the whole income; taxable income as gross income less exemptions and deductions, with the standard deduction and rebates applied before the slabs; the effective rate as tax ÷ gross income and the marginal rate as the rate on the next unit, which sets the value of a deduction; card interest by the average daily balance method at the monthly rate r ÷ 12; and a credit score as a weighted sum of payment history (commonly about 35%), amounts owed (about 30%), length of history (about 15%), new credit (about 10%) and credit mix (about 10%)

### Game engines and real-time rendering

- **Game loop and engine runtime architecture**: the loop of input, update and render once per frame; engine subsystems (resources, world, renderer, physics, audio, scripting) and their startup and shutdown order; a fixed simulation step decoupled from a variable render rate
- **Entity-component object models**: entities as identifiers, components as plain data and systems as functions over sets of components; composition in place of deep inheritance; components packed in dense arrays for cache-friendly iteration; the trade-off against object hierarchies in gameplay code; events and messages between systems as queued records rather than direct calls, so that an emitting system does not depend on the systems that consume its events
- **Memory management for engines**: per-frame linear (bump) allocators reset each frame, pool allocators for fixed-size objects and stack allocators for scoped work; fragmentation and the cost of general-purpose allocation inside the frame loop; the frame budget as the constraint that makes allocation-free paths necessary
- **Resource handles, reference counting and virtual filesystems**: a resource is referred to by a handle, an index into a table the engine owns, not by a raw pointer, so the engine can move, reload or free the object, and a generation counter stored with each slot makes a stale handle detectable; reference counting frees a shared resource when its count of holders reaches zero, costing an increment and a decrement per copy and failing on cycles, which need a weak reference or a tracing sweep; a virtual filesystem maps logical paths onto mounted archives and directories in a fixed precedence order, so the game loads an asset by its logical name whatever package holds it
- **Skeletal animation**: a rig of joints in a hierarchy, each with a local transform; skinning vertices by weighted sums of joint matrices (linear blend skinning); keyframe interpolation with quaternion slerp for rotations; blending and state machines over clips; the candy-wrapper and volume-loss artifacts of linear blending
- **Collision detection and rigid-body dynamics**: broad phase by sweep-and-prune or bounding-volume hierarchies; narrow phase by GJK and separating-axis tests for convex shapes; contact manifolds; impulse-based resolution with restitution and friction; Newton's equations integrated at a fixed timestep, with tunnelling as the failure mode of large steps
- **Audio systems**: sample playback and mixing into a fixed-rate output buffer; the audio thread's real-time deadline and lock-free hand-off from the game thread; spatialization by distance attenuation, panning and Doppler; streaming of large assets and voice-count limits; latency as buffer size divided by sample rate
- **Input devices and game timing**: polling and event queues for keyboards, mice and gamepads; input latency measured from device to display; the high-resolution clock and frame timing; the fixed-timestep accumulator that runs zero or more simulation steps per rendered frame and interpolates state between them
- **Game random numbers**: a seeded pseudorandom generator, such as xorshift or PCG, gives the same sequence from the same seed, so a recorded seed with the recorded inputs replays the same game state; gameplay randomness uses its own generator, kept apart from the one used for cosmetic effects, so a cosmetic change cannot alter gameplay outcomes; the generator's period must exceed the number of draws in a session, and its small state and fast step suit per-frame use
- **Graphics pipeline**: vertex processing through model, view and projection transforms; primitive assembly, clipping, perspective division and viewport mapping; rasterization into fragments, the depth test and blending; the GPU as parallel stages; a frame as a sequence of draw calls and state changes
- **GPGPU**: compute shaders dispatch a grid of thread groups that run the same kernel on different data, so a per-particle or per-cell update maps to one thread per element; threads in a group share local memory and synchronize with barriers; the main cost is moving data between CPU and GPU memory, paid per upload and per readback, so results the CPU needs every frame are the expensive case
- **Shading and BRDFs**: the rendering equation; a bidirectional reflectance distribution function f(ωᵢ, ωₒ) and its energy conservation; Lambertian diffuse and Blinn–Phong specular; per-vertex versus per-pixel shading (Gouraud and Phong interpolation); the Cook–Torrance microfacet model with distribution, geometry and Fresnel terms
- **Texturing and mipmaps**: texture coordinates by barycentric interpolation; nearest, bilinear and trilinear filtering; mipmap chains costing about one third more memory; level of detail from the screen-space derivatives of texture coordinates; anisotropic filtering against blur at grazing angles
- **Shadow maps**: rendering depth from the light's view; comparing each fragment's light-space depth with the stored depth under a bias; shadow acne and peter-panning as the bias failure modes; percentage-closer filtering for soft edges; cascaded shadow maps splitting the view frustum to keep resolution near the camera
- **Physically based rendering**: energy-conserving BRDFs parameterized by albedo, metalness and roughness; the microfacet specular term; image-based lighting from a prefiltered environment map for diffuse and specular; the split-sum approximation for real-time specular; the linear-light pipeline in which textures and lighting are converted and combined before display
- **Global illumination**: indirect light as the recursive term of the rendering equation; light baking into lightmaps and probes; screen-space and voxel approximations; ambient occlusion as a visibility term; the cost of each approximation and its failures where information is missing, such as off-screen geometry
- **Image-space effects (tone mapping, depth of field, motion blur)**: tone mapping of high-dynamic-range radiance into display range with exposure and a curve such as Reinhard or an ACES-style filmic curve; depth of field from the circle of confusion computed from depth; motion blur from a per-pixel velocity buffer; the post-processing chain as full-screen passes and their bandwidth cost
- **Ray tracing and BVH acceleration**: ray–primitive intersection; bounding volume hierarchies built by median or surface-area-heuristic splits, traversed in about logarithmic time per ray; the slab test for axis-aligned boxes; hardware ray tracing with denoising for real-time use; the rasterization-versus-tracing trade-off
- **Level of detail and culling**: view-frustum culling against bounding volumes; occlusion culling with hierarchical depth buffers; backface culling; discrete LOD switching by distance with hysteresis and continuous LOD by geometry morphing; a screen-space error metric that decides when to switch
- **Virtual and augmented reality**: stereo rendering from two eye viewpoints with asymmetric projection frusta; the motion-to-photon latency budget, a commonly quoted target of about 20 ms to be checked against the headset vendor's figures, and time-warp reprojection to hide late frames; inside-out and outside-in tracking; passthrough and anchoring for augmented reality
- **Curves and splines**: parametric curves; Bézier curves evaluated by de Casteljau's algorithm; Catmull–Rom and cubic Hermite splines through control points; B-splines with local support over a knot vector; tangent continuity (C1) and curvature continuity (C2) at the joins; their use in paths, animation curves and surfaces
- **Asset pipeline**: import of source art (meshes, textures, audio) into engine formats; cooking of textures into GPU block-compressed formats with mipmaps generated at build time; dependency graphs between assets so that a change rebuilds only the outputs that depend on it; content hashes of the sources for incremental builds; streaming chunks sized to per-platform memory budgets
- **Level streaming**: the world is split into cells, and a cell loads when the player comes within a load radius and unloads beyond a larger unload radius; the gap between the two radii is hysteresis, which stops a cell loading and unloading as the player walks along its border; loading runs on a background thread under a per-frame time budget, and the policy must finish a cell before the player can reach it or the frame stalls; the cost is memory for the cells in the window and the I/O bandwidth their assets need
- **Engine tools and iteration**: scene editors with undo, hot reload of scripts and shaders, CPU timelines and GPU timers in profilers, and frame capture for inspecting a single frame's draw calls; the edit-to-result time as the measure of iteration speed; scripting layers that call engine functions through bindings; data definitions in versioned text formats so that tools and runtime read the same schema
- **Script bindings and leak detection**: scripts reach engine functions through a binding layer that checks arguments and converts values between script and engine types, with engine-owned objects passed as handles so a script cannot hold a dangling pointer; each crossing has a cost, so per-frame calls are batched; a debug build records each allocation with its size and call site and reports blocks still live at shutdown or growing across frames, and guard bytes around each block catch overruns
- **Volumetric and translucent rendering**: transmittance along a ray T = exp(−∫σ(s) ds) by the Beer–Lambert law, with in-scattering added along the path for fog and light shafts; the over operator with premultiplied alpha, whose result depends on draw order; order-independent transparency by weighted blended accumulation; view-aligned froxel grids as the volume that stores scattering and density per depth slice
- **Non-photorealistic rendering**: stylized shading by quantizing the diffuse term into a few bands along a toon ramp; outlines by the inverted-hull method, which expands back faces along their normals, or by edge detection on depth and normal buffers; hatching patterns in screen or texture space driven by the lighting value; the same geometry and passes as physically based rendering with a different shading function

### Exercises

- From scratch: a software rasterizer that transforms triangles, clips them against the view frustum, rasterizes with edge functions, interpolates depth and barycentric attributes, and writes the frame to a PPM image checked against a known scene
- From scratch: a ray tracer with a bounding volume hierarchy built by a surface-area split, its ray-primitive test count compared against a brute-force loop over the same scene
- From scratch: an entity-component game loop with a fixed-timestep physics step, an accumulator and interpolated rendering, with particles colliding with boxes, and a test that replays a recorded input sequence to the same state
- **Reasoning drills**
  - Frame-budget arithmetic: at 60 frames per second a frame has about 16.7 ms; split that budget across input, simulation, culling, draw submission and GPU time, and predict which stage a given scene size breaks first
  - Choosing a culling strategy: for a scene of a given size and occluder density, choose between frustum culling alone, occlusion culling and a spatial index, and state the expected visible-object count before measuring
